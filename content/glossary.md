# Glossary

Key terms used in the workshop, in plain language. In the tool, the first time a term appears in a
step it's underlined; select it to see the definition. "Also matches" lists other forms of the term
the tool underlines; "(glossary only)" terms are too common to underline and appear only here.

| Term | Also matches | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| A/V/E script | A/V/E scripts | The Learning Media Team's script template for videos recorded on the XR stage, in three columns: Dialogue (Audio), the words you'll say; Graphics (Video), what appears on screen; and Environment, the virtual background behind you. |
| AI risk | | How easily AI could do an activity for a student. A high-risk activity is redesigned so students' own judgment shows, or AI use is built into the task. |
| Alt text | | A short written description of an image, chart, or diagram that a screen reader reads aloud, so students who can't see it get the same information. |
| Asynchronous | | Work students do on their own schedule within the week, such as readings, videos, and assignments, as opposed to a live session. |
| Backward design | planned backward | Planning a course from the end: first what students should be able to do, then how you'll see it, then what they'll read, watch, and practice to get there (Wiggins & McTighe, 2005). |
| Bloom's taxonomy | | A framework that sorts learning by how much thinking it asks of students, from remembering facts to creating something new. CLOs aim at the higher levels: apply, analyze, evaluate, and create. |
| Catalog commitments | catalog commitment | Each separate thing the official catalog description promises students will learn to do. Your CLOs are checked against them. |
| Character profile | character | The person students follow through the course: a premise statement, their position, the conflict they face, their peers, their values and fears, and a short backstory. |
| Check-in | check-ins, weekly check-in | Your weekly meeting with your ID, on Zoom or in person, to review the module you just finished. |
| CLO | CLOs, course-level objective, course-level objectives | Course-level objective: what students will be able to do by the end of the course, written so you can observe and grade it. A course has 3 to 6. |
| Cohort session | cohort sessions | A 90-minute live class on Zoom, held each week in Modules 2 to 8 in most Flex Online courses. Attendance is optional, and every session is recorded. |
| Confidence monitor | | A screen on the XR stage, out of the camera's view, that shows your slides and notes while you teach. |
| Conflict | (glossary only)
| Course Map | Course Maps | The finished, detailed record of everything in an existing Flex Online course, complete enough to rebuild it. Refresh courses upload theirs in step GS.2. |
| Course Planning Document | | The working plan you build in this workshop, a section at a time. Your ID and the development team build the course from it. |
| Curated material | curated reading, curated video | A reading or video from another source that you choose for your course, such as an article, chapter, or video. Curated is the default; it's the least work to produce. |
| Environment | (glossary only)
| Flex Online | | Mays Business School's online course format: a shared template, weekly modules, asynchronous work, and, in most courses, weekly live cohort sessions. |
| Full build | | The workshop track for a new course, or a course rebuilt from the beginning. Each step drafts a new section. |
| Gem | Gems, Gemini Gem | A custom Google Gemini assistant you set up with instructions and files. You can add a Gem to a Canvas module for students to use. |
| Grading assistant | | An AI assistant, built in TAMU AI Chat, that drafts rubric-based feedback on student work for you to review and edit. It never assigns the grade. |
| Guardrails | | What an AI assistant won't do, such as write a student's graded work or assign a final grade. |
| Guided practice | | Part of a cohort session where you work one example with the class: you lead, students supply the next step, and you coach as they go. |
| Hallucination | | When AI states something false with confidence. It's why you test what you build and check what AI drafts. |
| ID | your ID, IDs, instructional designer | Your instructional designer: the person from Mays Teaching & Learning Innovation who works with you through the workshop and development. |
| Instructional Technologist | | A member of the Learning Delivery team who sets up your Zoom sessions with you before the term and co-hosts each cohort session, running polls, breakouts, and timers. |
| Instructor-authored reading | instructor-authored readings | A reading you write yourself, usually 4 to 10 pages, when nothing curated fits or your own experience is the point. |
| Knowledge files | | Documents you attach to an AI assistant so its answers fit your course, such as assignment directions, your scenario, or a rubric. |
| Learning Delivery | | The Mays Teaching & Learning Innovation team that supports live teaching, including your cohort sessions and practice on the XR stage. |
| Learning Media Team | | The Mays Teaching & Learning Innovation team that records, edits, and produces your course's video, audio, and graphics. |
| Lesson plan | lesson plans, Cohort Session Lesson Plan | Your minute-by-minute plan for a cohort session, in the team's template: each section with its minutes and talking points, plus materials and room setup. |
| Module | (glossary only) | One week of a Flex Online course. Most courses have eight. |
| One-Touch studio | One-Touch studios | A small studio where you record screen-recorded videos on your own, to illustrate a process or teach with your slides. |
| Performance | (glossary only)
| Perusall | | A tool for social reading: students annotate a reading together, and you can reply in their threads. |
| Program outcomes | program outcome, PLO, PLOs | What students can do after the whole degree. Your course supports two to four of them. |
| Recording test | | The question every cohort session has to pass: if a student watched only the recording, what would they have missed? If the answer is nothing, they'll watch it later. |
| Refresh | Refresh track | The workshop track for a course already developed and taught in Flex Online. Each step reviews your current section and suggests updates. |
| RSI | regular and substantive interaction | Regular and substantive interaction: the federal requirement that the instructor actively teaches an online course, through instruction, feedback, and discussion on a predictable schedule. |
| Rubric | rubrics | A scoring guide that describes what each level of performance looks like for each part of an assignment. |
| Run of Show | XR Run of Show | The production team's plan for each cohort session broadcast from the XR stage. You fill in the session details, your background, and the assets you'll use. |
| Scenario | scenarios | A specific moment in a module when your character has to make a decision that calls for that module's CLOs. |
| Speaker notes | | Notes under each slide that only you see, used here for your talking points. |
| Storyline premise | storyline | One sentence that sums up your course's story: who the character is, what they want, and what stands in the way. |
| Student-facing outline | | A short outline of a cohort session for students: its main topics, discussion questions, and what's due. |
| System instructions | | The standing rules that tell an AI assistant who it is, what to do, and how to respond in every conversation. |
| TAMU AI Chat | | Texas A&M's AI chat tool. It offers several AI models in a university-protected environment. |
| Workload | week check | The hours a module asks of students in a week: activities, readings and media, and the cohort session. The target is about 4 hours in Module 1 and close to 10 in the rest. |
| Writer/Producer | | The Learning Media Team member who gets your scripts ready for production. |
| XR stage | | A studio with an LED wall that places you in a virtual setting. You record videos and broadcast cohort sessions from it. |
