## 7.1 Cohort session activities for Modules 2 to 8

Time: about 30 minutes, about 4 minutes a module
Pattern: pick and combine, one module at a time (full build); edit in place (refresh)

### Learn

::: purpose
**What you'll do:** Choose the activity at the heart of each cohort session, a 90-minute live class
on Zoom, for Modules 2 to 8: something students can only get by being there.

**Why it matters:** Students don't skip sessions because they're recorded; they skip sessions that
offer nothing the recording doesn't (Morris et al., 2019).
:::

Most Flex Online courses hold cohort sessions, but a few don't. Does yours?

::: choose cohort
- yes | Yes, it has cohort sessions | A 90-minute live session each week in Modules 2 to 8. Most Flex Online courses have them.
- no | No cohort sessions | Students work on their own schedule every week.
:::

Flex Online courses hold one cohort session each week from Module 2 to Module 8; Module 1 has none.
Attendance is optional and every session is recorded, so students decide each week whether to come
or watch later. In this step you choose each session's main activity. In step 7.2 you'll plan one
session minute by minute.

**The recording test.** Ask: if a student watched only the recording, what would they have
missed? If the answer is nothing, they'll watch it later. A session passes when at least one
part depends on who's in the room. Students get five things live that a recording can't give
them:

| What students get live | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Feedback on their own work | Students bring a draft of the next assignment and get responses |
| Applying the concept to their own job | Students map the week's framework onto a problem at work |
| Practice with peers | Students rehearse a conversation, pitch, or negotiation in breakouts |
| Your judgment on a messy case | You work through a case with no clean answer and explain your reasoning |
| A preview of the next assessment | Students work a problem shaped like the next assignment with you there |

**When your session meets.** Some cohort sessions are early in the week, on Monday or Tuesday;
others are later, on Wednesday or Thursday. The day changes what students have done before they
arrive, what the session is for, and how the rest of the week is laid out around it.

| | Early in the week (Monday or Tuesday) | Later in the week (Wednesday or Thursday) |
|---|---|---|
| What students have done | Little of this week's work yet | The readings, and they've started the activities |
| What the session is for | Close last week and launch this one | Practice and decide with what they've learned |
| How the week builds | The materials and activities follow the session, toward Sunday's assignment | The session is the rehearsal before Sunday's assignment |

**Choose your session day.** If it isn't set yet, choose the day you'd like and design around it;
your ID can confirm it with the program. You can change it later, and the AI's drafts follow what
you choose.

::: choose cohortDay
- mon | Monday | Early in the week: students haven't started this week's work.
- tue | Tuesday | Early in the week: students haven't started this week's work.
- wed | Wednesday | Later in the week: students have read the material.
- thu | Thursday | Later in the week: students have read and started the activities.
:::

::: when cohortDay=
Choose a day above to see the kind of activity that fits it.
:::

::: when cohortDay=wed,thu
**Your story as a live decision.** Let the week's materials carry the scenario up to the
decision, then stop. The session's activity is where students make the call: vote on the
character's options and argue the split, role-play the hard conversation in threes (one observer
gives hesitant students a way in), or give each group a different piece of evidence so the
picture only comes together in the debrief.
:::

::: when cohortDay=mon,tue
**Your story as a first call.** The session opens the story. Its activity is an introductory one:
students meet the character's new problem together and make a first call, list what they'd need
to know, or try the new skill once with your coaching. The week's materials then give them what
they need to test that call before Sunday's assignment.
:::

**RSI.** A session built this way is facilitated discussion (category 4) every week, often with
feedback (2) or direct instruction (1) as well.

## 7.2 Cohort session lesson plan

Time: about 40 minutes
Pattern: interview, then draft; then the AI drafts the outline and slides

### Learn

::: purpose
**What you'll do:** Plan your first cohort session, usually Module 2, minute by minute in the
team's lesson plan template. From your plan, the AI drafts the student-facing outline and a slide
deck.

**Why it matters:** A lesson plan keeps a 90-minute session on time and on purpose. Drafting the
outline and slides from the same plan means all three say the same thing.
:::

Every cohort session has three documents, each from a team template:

| Document | Who it's for | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| Cohort Session Lesson Plan | You | Title, overview, objectives, and main topics; how many students and how the breakouts are set up; each section with its minutes and talking points; and the materials, handouts, and room setup you need |
| Student-facing outline | Students | A welcome, the main topics, each topic's main points and discussion questions, and a wrap-up with what's due |
| Slides (Flex Online template) | Everyone in the session | A title slide, the agenda, a section slide for each topic, a slide for each poll or question, breakout instructions, and a wrap-up, with your talking points as speaker notes |

You write the lesson plan. The AI drafts the outline and slides from it, and you edit them.

::: when cohortDay=wed,thu,
**A shape for later in the week.** Students arrive having read the material:

1. **Check understanding (about 10 minutes).** Open with a poll or a few chat questions on the
   week's main concepts. It shows you what students took from the materials, and you can clear up
   a misunderstanding before it spreads. Keep it short; don't re-teach the reading.
2. **Guided practice (about 15 minutes).** Work one example with the class. You lead, students
   supply the next step or the next move, and you coach as they go.
3. **The live activity (most of the session).** Students practice or decide in small groups: the
   activity you chose in step 7.1.
4. **Debrief and preview.** Groups share, you add your judgment, and you point to what's due next.
:::

::: when cohortDay=mon,tue
**A shape for early in the week.** Students arrive with last week fresh and this week unread:

1. **Recap last week (about 15 minutes).** Revisit last week's decision and what you saw in
   students' work, and connect it to what comes next. A poll on last week's main idea eases
   students in.
2. **Introduce the new module (about 15 minutes).** Present the character's new problem and the
   week's main ideas in brief, so students know what the materials will help them answer.
3. **The introductory activity (most of the session).** The first call you chose in step 7.1, in
   small groups, with your coaching.
4. **Preview the week.** Point to the materials, what's due, and how today's first call feeds
   Sunday's assignment.
:::

Either way, lecturing on new material belongs in the asynchronous content.

**Quiet rooms.** Over a third of distance learners feel stressed when asked to participate live
(Donelan et al., 2025). The opening poll eases them in: start with a poll or the chat, then small
groups, then the whole room.

**Who runs Zoom with you.** An Instructional Technologist is a member of the Learning Delivery team
assigned to your cohort sessions. In the weeks before the term, they meet with you to set up your
Zoom meetings: the settings, polls, pre-assigned breakout rooms, captions, and recording. During
each session they're your co-host: they admit students, launch polls, open and close breakouts,
and keep time, so you can focus on teaching. They can only set up what's in the plan, so your
lesson plan spells out each poll's exact wording and options, each breakout's size and length, and
anything to post in the chat. Your ID passes the plan along.

**Accessible slides.** Students join from many devices, and some use screen readers, magnifiers, or
captions. The Flex Online slide template handles much of this; the rest is up to you:

- **Use the template's layouts.** Each slide's title and text boxes set the order a screen reader
  reads, and text boxes you add yourself may be read out of order or skipped. Give every slide its
  own title.
- **Keep text readable.** Keep the template's fonts and colors. If you change a color, keep the
  contrast between text and background at 4.5:1 or more (3:1 for large text), and don't use color
  alone to show meaning.
- **Describe images.** Add alt text to every image, chart, or diagram that teaches something: what
  it shows and why it matters. Mark decorative images as decorative.
- **Use real text.** Type text on the slide instead of using a picture of text, and use link text
  that says where the link goes instead of a bare web address.
- **Say what's on the slide.** Read poll questions aloud and say what a chart shows, so students
  who are listening, or watching the recording later, don't miss it.
- **Check it.** In PowerPoint, select **Review**, then **Check Accessibility**. The checker flags
  most problems before you share the deck with your ID.

During development you'll plan the rest of your sessions with your ID.

## 7.3 Ready for the stage

Time: about 15 minutes
Pattern: check and complete

### Learn

::: purpose
**What you'll do:** Choose your background and list the assets for broadcasting your first cohort
session from the XR stage.

**Why it matters:** Cohort sessions are broadcast live from the XR stage. Your ID takes your
background and assets to the team that sets up the stage, Zoom, polls, and breakouts before you go
live.
:::

The **XR Run of Show** is the production team's plan for the day: call times, who's on the crew, a
pre-show checklist, and a backup plan. It sets the order of the session but leaves your teaching
unscripted. You give your ID two things for it: your **background environment** and your **session
assets**, such as slides, polls, and handouts, in the order you'll use them.

**Keep it simple.** Choose one background with the Learning Media Team; the one you chose for your
videos works well. Teach with your slides from step 7.2, and post handouts in Canvas so a link can
go in the chat.

**On the day.** Arrive at the call time the team gives you for a mic check and a walkthrough. Look
at the camera lens when you speak to students; a confidence monitor shows your slides and notes.
The team gives you time signals, and your Instructional Technologist runs Zoom, so you can focus on
teaching.

Before your first session, schedule a practice session on the stage with Learning Delivery. It's
the time to try the polls and breakouts, find where to look, and get comfortable with the setting.
